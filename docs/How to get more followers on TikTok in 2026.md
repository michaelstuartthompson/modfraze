How to gain followers on TikTok: Proven strategies that work

1. Use the Hook-Value-Loop framework
The first three seconds of any TikTok video decide whether the rest gets watched and pushed to more For You pages. The Hook-Value-Loop framework gives you a structure that the algorithm rewards, helping you expand your organic reach. 

Here’s how each part works.

Hook (zero to three seconds): Open with a bold claim, a question, a visual surprise, or a pattern interrupt, so a scrolling viewer has a reason to stop.
Value: Deliver the promise of the hook fast – a tip, a reveal, or a punchline.
Loop: End in a way that makes the video restart cleanly. Loops drive rewatches, which is a strong watch time signal to send to the algorithm.
Check out this example for a print-on-demand seller running a graphic tee shop.

Hook: “This $19 shirt got more compliments than anything in my closet.”
Value: Show the custom t-shirt, a close-up of the design, and a quick try-on shot.
Loop: Cut back to the opening line with “Which is exactly why…” When TikTok auto-replays, the opening line resumes where it left off, and the viewer rewatches without noticing.
2. Mix trend, value, and conversion videos
A feed that feels too salesy is one of the fastest ways to drive away TikTok users. Avoid this by rotating through three content types.

Trend-based videos: Hop on a trending song, a viral moment, or a challenge that fits your niche.
Value videos: Build trust through educational videos, behind-the-scenes content, or useful tips. 
Conversion videos: Show the product, the unique selling point, or the use case so viewers want to follow or even purchase.
Let’s say you’re starting a tote bag business.

Trend-based: “What’s in my bag” with a trending sound featuring your hero design. 
Value: A “how to wash a printed tote without fading the design” tutorial. 
Conversion: A breakdown of your new tote bag’s design, print quality, and features.
After cycling through these videos, check your TikTok analytics and double down on which content type pulls the most new followers.

3. Post for volume first, then optimize

Creators publishing six to 10 new videos per week get 29%more views than once-a-week posters. 

That’s because the more video content you post, the more material theTikTok app has to push to potential followers and boost your content’s visibility. 

Don’t worry about video quality too much at this point. The TikTok algorithm rewardsauthenticity over polish, so a phone camera, a clear hook, and a quick edit will beat a slick studio shoot. 

Treat each post as an experiment and test different video lengths, content ideas, formats, hooks, sounds, and angles. Change one variable at a time to see what’s working in your TikTok strategy.

4. Create content that people want to share
When many users share your video, the app treats it as proof your content connects with the right audience – and pushes it harder on the For You page.

To create engaging videos that get shared, lean into one of these four content buckets.

Relatable: “This is so me” videos that resonate with viewers. A phone case shop, for example, can post “POV: 2 am, you, doomscrolling again” with the case clearly visible in the shot.
Useful: Tips, checklists, and “do this instead” videos that solve your target audience’s problems. Answer questions buyers ask in your direct messages or comments that no one else in your niche is discussing. 
Surprising: A reveal, transformation, or unexpected fact that gives viewers a reason to react. For a POD seller, that could be a “you won’t believe what this custom hoodie costs” breakdown.
Emotional: Humor, nostalgia, and inspiration trigger shares. The story of why you started your brand or a customer message that made your day are solid examples.
End your video with a line like “send this to a friend who’d love this” or “tag the person you’d buy this for” to nudge viewers to share your content. 

5. Reply to comments with videos
Video replies let you skip the hardest part of content creation – figuring out what to post – because a viewer just told you. 

Here are three types of comments worth turning into video replies.

Repeat questions: Reply to these concerns with a single comprehensive video. Pin it to your TikTok profile so the post keeps getting views. 
Concerns or criticisms: A video that addresses doubt head-on builds more trust than a hundred polished product shots. 
Funny or unexpected comments. When someone leaves a comment that makes you laugh or stops you in your tracks, that’s a hook handed to you for free. Reply with a video that runs with the joke. 
6. Collaborate with other creators
A joyful young couple in colorful sweaters takes a selfie with a phone on a selfie stick, both smiling widely.
Tapping into another creator’s audience is one of the best ways to gain TikTok followers fast. TikTok has three formats built for it – stitches, duets, and collabs. Use them to make content that brings their viewers to you.

Stitch lets you clip up to five seconds of another video and add your own commentary. You can stitch someone else’s video to reach their viewers, or post stitch-bait – a bold opinion or “tell me I’m wrong” prompt – and let other creators bring their audiences back to your original post.

Duet plays your video side by side with another creator, which works well for reactions and expert breakdowns.

Collabs are the next step up. Find creators in the same niche and pitch a partnership – a co-created video, a giveaway, a product review, or a live stream where they host and feature your products to their followers.

Here’s an idea – send a TikTok influencer a free product, ask them to film a styling or unboxing video, and stitch or duet their post to cross-promote your profiles. Both audiences see the exchange, and your brand picks up viewers from the original creator.

7. Use TikTok SEO to increase followers
Users type queries into the TikTok search bar the same way they’d Google something, and the algorithm pulls up videos that match. 

Here’s how to use TikTok SEO to pull in new followers.

Find the right keywords with Creator Search Insights

Creator Search Insights is a tool inside the TikTok app. It shows what people are actually searching for in your niche – the queries getting volume, the topics with low creator coverage, and the trending angles you can jump on. 

It’s also one of the fastest ways to source video ideas, since every high-volume search is just content waiting to be made.

Place keywords where the algorithm reads them

Once you’ve got two or three target phrases per video, weave them into the spots TikTok actually scans:

Caption
On-screen text
Voiceover
Repeating the same phrase in all three spots tells the algorithm exactly what your video is about and pushes it toward viewers searching for that topic.

Leverage trending hashtags strategically 

The right hashtags are the most relevant ones. 

TikTok cross-checks your hashtags against your video’s audio, captions, and on-screen text. When they don’t match, the algorithm might read the post as spam, and your reach takes the hit.

Instead of the broad #fyp, use one or two relevant hashtags that genuinely fit your video and one or two niche hashtags your target audience already follows.

8. Use viral TikTok hooks to get more followers
A smiling man with headphones in a yellow shirt holds a smartphone on a selfie stick, gesturing and looking at the camera.
A strong hook turns a passive scroller into an active viewer. Here are some hook templates that work across niches.

“Three things I’d never [do/use/charge for] as a [job title].” Share insider rules from your niche. 
“After [specific number] [orders/clients/years], here’s what I learned.” Authority hook for any business with a solid track record. 
“I almost didn’t sell this [product].” A vulnerability hook for new product drops.
“I tested every [category] under $[price] so you don’t have to.” Comparison hooks earn watch time because viewers want to know what comes out on top. 
“Stop scrolling if you’ve ever [done X].” Speaks directly to the viewer, prompting them to stop and check whether the video is for them.
“Watch this before you [common mistake].” Warning hooks land because they imply you’re saving the viewer time, money, or their reputation. 
“This is the [tip/trick/idea] I wish someone had told me at [age/stage].” Reflective hook for creators sharing knowledge.

9. Use TikTok analytics to grow faster
Once you’ve got multiple videos up, use TikTok analytics to figure out what’s actually pulling new followers. To spot your top-performing videos, check these metrics.

Follower conversion: See which videos gained you followers over a certain period. Those are the ones building your account.
Average watch time: A high number means viewers are sticking around past your hook. 
Retention rate: TikTok shows you exactly where viewers drop off. Figure out what exactly is causing each dip. Better retention earns more reach, and more reach earns more followers.
Once you’ve identified these videos, look for the pattern. Is it the product type? Hook style? Length? Whatever the common thread is, build your next batch of high-quality content around it. 

It’s all about giving your target audience what they’re looking for. 

Why are you not getting more followers on TikTok?
If you’ve tried the tactics above and still don’t know how to get people to follow you on TikTok, it likely comes down to one of these issues. 

Weak hooks
If more than half your viewers drop off before the three-second mark, that means your hook is not strong enough. 

Find your videos with the steepest early drop-off, and rewrite their first line. Sometimes the fix is as small as moving the payoff to an earlier timestamp.

No clear niche
Posting a cooking video one day, a finance meme the next, and a vague personal vlog after that can confuse the algorithm. 

Pick one niche and stay in it. Variety should live in the format – tutorials, trends, behind-the-scenes – not in the subject matter.

Overly polished content
Polished videos underperform on TikTok because they come across as ads. Short-form video content that thrives on the platform feels authentic, fast, and a little spontaneous. 

Repurposing content from other platforms rarely works either. A clip cut from a YouTube video has the wrong pacing, aspect ratio, and tone for the For You page. 

Inconsistent posting
One week of seven videos followed by three weeks of silence trains the algorithm to deprioritize your account. Several videos a week, every week, beats a burst of effort followed by a vanishing act. 

Too salesy
New accounts that lead with “buy this” before they’ve earned attention rarely convert viewers, because no one knows them yet. 

Build a backlog of value, entertainment, or personality first. The selling formats work better once viewers have a reason to trust you – which should come from your existing video content, not from your bio.

Buying fake followers
Bought followers don’t watch, like, or comment. The moment you have 10,000 of them and your videos still get zero engagement, the algorithm reads the gap and assumes your content isn’t worth pushing. 

As a result, genuine followers you would’ve earned organically never see your posts.
