Understand your current engagement metrics
A woman seated at a desk, focused on her laptop, with a notepad and pen beside her.
Before you can increase engagement on TikTok, you need to understand the core TikTok metrics – views, watch time, likes, comments, shares, and saves. They reveal how well your videos capture attention and connect with your target audience.

To calculate your TikTok engagement rate, use this formula:

(Likes + comments + shares) ÷ views × 100
For instance, if your post earns 500 likes, 100 comments, and 50 shares from 10,000 views, your engagement rate is 6.5%. 

Tracking this helps you identify the type of TikTok content that resonates most.

Use TikTok Analytics or TikTok Studio to compare your top-performing posts and find opportunities to boost engagement. Generally, a good TikTok engagement rate for smaller accounts is between 5-10%, while larger ones average 1-3%.

Use the TikTok Creator Portal and advanced features like creator tools to refine your results and strengthen your overall TikTok strategy.

Understand how the TikTok algorithm works
A person sitting on a bed, holding a cell phone displaying the TikTok logo.
The system uses AI to track what users watch, skip, or interact with, then serves similar content on the For You page. Your goal is simple: create TikTok videos that keep viewers watching.

Here’s what to optimize for:

Retention. The algorithm favors videos watched to the end. “Design with me” clips work well because viewers stay to see the final result. Loop-friendly content and strong endings both help.
Relevance. Use niche-specific hashtags, keywords, and trending sounds that align with your target audience.
Engagement. Encourage viewers to Duet, Stitch, or leave comments – these are strong signals that your TikTok content resonates.
When your content creation keeps people watching and interacting, TikTok rewards you with more reach and visibility.

Create engaging content that resonates
A woman sitting on the floor, holding a phone in one hand and a camera in the other, looking thoughtful.
To get more engagement on TikTok, focus on social media content that feels real and easy to relate to. Show your product in action instead of relying on static visuals. Film someone wearing or using it in everyday situations so viewers can picture themselves with it.

Share unboxings, sample orders, or quick behind-the-scenes clips to build trust and spark curiosity. The easier it is to visualize the product, the more likely people are to engage. These are some of the highest-performing content formats for print-on-demand sellers specifically.

Every scroll is a split-second choice, so your content needs to grab attention fast. For print-on-demand sellers, that means creating clips that feel authentic, valuable, and relevant to your audience.

Here’s how to make your content work harder for maximum engagement and TikTok success:

Write captions that earn the click. Your caption is a second hook – use it to tease, ask a question, or set up the payoff viewers have to watch for.
Use sound intentionally. Trending sounds boost discoverability, but the sound should match your video’s mood – a mismatch kills retention.
Build a content arc. Even a 15-second clip should have a beginning, middle, and payoff. Viewers who reach the end are your strongest engagement signal.
Deliver value or emotion. Share quick tips, relatable moments, or transformations that highlight your product.
Encourage viewer interaction. Ask simple questions like “Which design should I print next?” to drive more comments.
Mix your content formats. Rotate between user-generated content (UGC), tutorials, behind-the-scenes clips, and storytelling to keep your feed fresh and engaging.
Consistently posting relevant content that resonates with viewers signals to the TikTok algorithm that your videos deserve wider reach.

Optimize your posting strategy and format
Consistency is key to boosting your engagement on TikTok. The more regularly you post, the more data the TikTok algorithm collects to understand your style, audience, and performance, helping your future content reach more people. 

Most small brands and creators see steady growth by posting 3-5 times per week, experimenting with different content types and publishing times.

The best time to post on TikTok for engagement varies depending on your audience’s location and habits. Scheduled posting at peak hours – early mornings (6-9 am) and evenings (7-10 pm) – performs best. 

Use TikTok Analytics or scheduling tools like Later, SocialBee, or TikTok Studio to identify when your followers are most active and plan your content accordingly.

Make your videos stand out and drive engagement with these tips:

Keep them short and snappy. Aim for 15-30 seconds, long enough to share your message but short enough to maintain watch time (one of the strongest engagement signals).
Stick to vertical format. TikTok is built for mobile viewing, so always shoot vertically to fill the screen.
Use strong video captions and TikTok SEO keywords. Use phrases your target audience searches for, such as “custom t-shirt design tutorial” or “small business packaging tips,” to improve discoverability.
Add relevant hashtags. Combine broad trending hashtags (#SmallBusinessTikTok or #MadeWithPrintify) with niche, product-specific ones. Avoid stuffing too many – 3-5 is usually ideal.
On top of all that, optimize your TikTok profile. Use a clear profile picture, a recognizable TikTok handle, and a short, benefit-focused bio – something like “Y2K custom apparel | Ships worldwide” works well. Add links to your TikTok Shop or other social media platforms so interested viewers can easily explore your brand. 

A complete profile makes your content look professional and helps convert new viewers into loyal followers.

Build community and engagement
Business professionals in an office celebrating with hands raised in the air, expressing success and teamwork.
Building a strong community is what drives consistent TikTok growth. Content like unboxing reveals and real customer reactions works especially well because it feels authentic and easy to trust.

Showing someone opening your product, reacting to it, or using it for the first time helps viewers connect and imagine the same experience. That emotional connection can lead to a higher engagement rate.

Here’s how to turn that into a community:

Share unboxing and reaction videos. Film your own sample orders or repost customer reactions to build trust and excitement.
Reply and interact consistently. Respond to comments with text or video replies to keep conversations going.
Use Duets and Stitches. React to a customer’s unboxing, or Stitch a trending video and show how your product fits in.
Encourage UGC. Ask buyers to share their own hauls or styling videos and use a branded hashtag.
Go LIVE. TikTok Live lets you show products, answer questions, and preview new designs in real time – and the algorithm tends to boost accounts that go live regularly. Even a short live session once or twice a week can meaningfully increase your reach.
You don’t need a dedicated social media manager to make this work – consistency and timing matter more than resources.

The more your audience sees real people interacting with your products, the more likely they are to join in, helping you grow both engagement and loyalty.

Common pitfalls to avoid
Even the most creative TikTok strategy can fall flat if you overlook the basics. Here are the mistakes that hold small brands back – and how to fix them:

Posting inconsistently. Irregular uploads confuse the TikTok algorithm and make it harder for your target audience to find you. Pick a schedule you can realistically stick to – even 3 posts a week beats 10 posts one week and nothing the next.
Ignoring community interaction. If you’re not replying to comments, you’re leaving trust on the table. Engaging in your comments section – even briefly – signals authenticity and keeps your followers invested.
Copying TikTok trends without brand fit. Jumping on every viral content challenge might boost temporary views, but it won’t help your long-term TikTok engagement. Always ask: can I make this current viral trend work for my products? If not, skip it.
Focusing on vanity metrics. Likes and views matter, but they don’t always translate to sales or loyal TikTok followers. Track your TikTok engagement rate – it tells you whether people actually connect with your content.
Using irrelevant or excessive hashtags. Stuffing 20 hashtags into a caption doesn’t help – it can actually limit your reach. Use 3-5 targeted tags: a mix of trending and niche.
Neglecting your profile setup. An incomplete TikTok profile or unclear bio can discourage new viewers from following you. Keep your bio clear, your visuals cohesive, and your handle consistent across other platforms.
Fix these, and you’ll immediately be ahead of most small brands on the platform.

Back your TikTok strategy with data
TikTok videos between 15 and 20 seconds earn the highest completion rates – and completion rate is one of the strongest signals you can send to the algorithm. Short and punchy isn’t just an aesthetic choice; it’s a strategic one.

Here’s how to make data work for you and boost engagement:

Track metrics weekly. Review your TikTok analytics to see which formats get more saves, comments, or shares.
Test variations. Adjust hooks, trending sounds, and captions to find what delivers higher engagement.
Refine your schedule. Tools like Later, Hopper HQ, and TikTok Studio reveal the best time to post on TikTok for engagement, and help you plan around peak hours.
Compare week over week. Look at what drove saves, shares, or rewatches – then do more of it.
Experiment with timing and topics. Test different posting times and explore trends that fit your brand voice. Patterns will emerge faster than you think.
These insights strengthen your TikTok marketing and help you build a successful TikTok strategy that expands your reach across other social media platforms, too. The best TikTok accounts aren’t just creative – they’re iterative. Every post is a data point. Use your analytics to spot what works, double down on it, and cut what doesn’t.
